# Item 4: trained draft head (EAGLE-3 style) for Qwen3.5-9B on a 24 GB M5: feasibility (no training done)

Sources with URLs and figures: `sources.md` (collected by a research subagent; [V] = read from the primary page).
I re-checked the two load-bearing ones myself on 2026-11-19: the AQ-MedAI Qwen3.5-397B EAGLE-3 card (acceptance table)
and FastMTP (arXiv 2509.18362, §3.1/§3.4). Both match.

## Verdict
- **EAGLE-3 head: NO-GO.**
- **Fine-tuning the shipped MTP head (FastMTP style): a small, bounded pilot is possible. Expected gain is modest and
  unproven for this model. Not a priority over DFlash and the pipelined MTP loop.**

## Why not EAGLE-3
1. **Published EAGLE-3 heads for Qwen3.5-class models do not beat the native MTP head.** Qwen3.5-397B, average
   acceptance length EAGLE-3 vs MTP: MT-bench 3.03 vs 3.28, GSM8K 3.40 vs 3.54, HumanEval 3.47 vs 3.62. MTP wins
   every task, and throughput is a wash (224.1 vs 224.9 tok/s).
2. **EAGLE-3's headline numbers rely on trees.** τ 6.23 and 4.44× on Llama-3.1-8B at T=0 come from a 60-token,
   depth-8 tree. On this machine trees don't pay:
   - widths above 5 rows hit the matmul cliff (§3);
   - branch drafting projects at most +7% here (§14.1);
   - GDN state rollback needs a snapshot per position, which tree branches cannot share (TreeWY, arXiv 2608.20961).
3. **Data is the dominant cost and does not fit an M5.**
   - EAGLE-3 trains on ShareGPT (~68K) plus UltraChat (~464K), with responses regenerated by the target.
   - At our 19.7 tok/s single-stream (pipelined, §14.3), 10M regenerated tokens take about 6 days and 100M about
     2 months.
4. **DFlash already ships a trained drafter for this exact model.** It measured 115 tok/s on code edits and 44–49 on
   math here (§13, §14.4). That is the "trained drafter" option, already available.

## The cheaper option: fine-tune the shipped MTP head
- **FastMTP** fine-tuned MiMo-7B's MTP head:
  - 389.4K self-distilled samples, 3 epochs, under 1 day on one H20 server;
  - acceptance went from ~70% / ~10% / ~0% to 80% / 56% / 36% at draft positions 1/2/3;
  - speedup went from 1.21× to 2.03× at K = 3.
- **Its gain came from a vanilla head that failed beyond position 1.** Ours does not:
  - our position-2 acceptance is 0.54 given position 1 (~0.38 overall) on chat, and 0.75 given position 1 on math
    (§14.1);
  - so the headroom here is smaller and mostly at long context, where p1 is 0.34–0.53.
- **Loss choice matters.** arXiv 2606.12370 (Qwen3.5-35A3B MTP) found an acceptance-aware TV loss beats cross-entropy
  by 2–8 acceptance points, and that cross-entropy alone can lower step-1 acceptance.

Memory on a 24 GB M5 (estimate):

| Part | GB |
|---|---|
| Frozen 4-bit target (or none, with cached hidden states) | 5.6 |
| MTP layer, trainable: ~0.2–0.4 B params, bf16 weights + fp32 Adam | 2–5 |
| Logits on the 32k sub-vocab (our draft head), 2k-token sequences, fp32 + grad | ~0.5 |
| **Total** | **~9–11** |

This fits under the 14 GB cap. With the full 248k vocab it does not fit without chunked cross-entropy (~1 MB of
logits per token).

Data on an M5:
- Hidden-state cache: the MTP head needs only the last hidden state, 4096 × bf16 = 8 KB per token, so 10M tokens is
  80 GB on disk.
- Generation is the bottleneck: ~6 days single-stream for 10M tokens, less with batched generation (not measured).

Throughput: no MLX or MPS training recipe for draft heads was found, so this is unmeasured. The only MLX EAGLE-3 work
is inference (Llama-8B, M3 Ultra, 1.05×).

**Proposed pilot, if wanted:**
1. Generate ~20K self-distilled samples (~10M tokens) at T = 0.6 from long-context and chat prompts.
2. Train the MTP layer only, with a TV/acceptance-aware loss on the 32k sub-vocab, for one epoch.
3. Measure p1/p2 with `research/branch/sim.py` (same harness, same prompts) before any end-to-end run.

Go/no-go for the pilot: p2 | p1 on chat ≥ 0.65 and p1 at 8k ≥ 0.60 (from 0.54 and 0.53). Below that, the expected
gain (≤ +10%) does not pay for a week of generation.
